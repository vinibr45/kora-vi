# Service Recovery Framework

## Summary

Service recovery is the structured response when a customer has a poor experience, confusion, delay, unmet expectation, or unresolved issue.

## Core Idea

Good recovery protects trust by combining acknowledgement, diagnosis, ownership, resolution, and prevention.

Use five steps:

1. Acknowledge the customer's experience.
2. Clarify what happened.
3. Own the next action.
4. Resolve or propose options.
5. Prevent recurrence when possible.

## When To Use

- Responding to complaints, delays, bugs, misunderstandings, missed expectations, or service failures.
- Creating support scripts, escalation workflows, and customer success processes.
- Reviewing whether a response is empathetic and useful.

## When Not To Use

- When legal, safety, medical, financial, or compliance constraints require a specialized response.
- When the customer is abusive and boundary-setting is required.
- When the issue requires technical investigation before a promise can be made.

## How To Apply

Response structure:

```text
Acknowledgement:
What we understand:
What we will check or do:
When we will update you:
What happens next:
```

Avoid blaming the customer, over-apologizing without action, promising what cannot be delivered, or hiding uncertainty.

## Examples

Weak response:

```text
Sorry for the inconvenience. We will check.
```

Better response:

```text
I understand the delivery delay disrupted your plan. I will check the current status now, confirm the next available date, and send you an update by 15:00 today.
```

## Limitations

Empathy cannot replace operational correction. If the same issue repeats, the workflow, product, expectation-setting, or capacity needs review.
