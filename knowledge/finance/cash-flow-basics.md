# Cash Flow Basics

## Summary

Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of a business over time. A profitable business can still suffer if cash arrives too late or expenses are concentrated too early.

## Core Idea

Cash flow should be read through timing, predictability, obligations, and runway.

Basic components:

- opening balance;
- expected inflows;
- expected outflows;
- fixed costs;
- variable costs;
- debt or tax obligations;
- closing balance;
- runway;
- risk events.

## When To Use

- Reviewing whether a business can afford a decision.
- Planning hiring, tools, marketing spend, inventory, or project delivery.
- Diagnosing financial stress despite revenue.
- Creating monthly management reports.

## When Not To Use

- As tax, accounting, investment, or legal advice.
- When accurate financial records are missing.
- When regulated financial planning is required.

## How To Apply

Use a simple monthly view:

```text
Opening cash:
Expected receipts:
Expected payments:
Fixed costs:
Variable costs:
Taxes/debt:
Closing cash:
Minimum safe reserve:
Risk notes:
```

Then ask:

1. Which expenses are unavoidable?
2. Which receipts are uncertain?
3. What happens if revenue is delayed?
4. What decision would improve the next 30 to 90 days?

## Examples

A business with strong sales but long payment terms may need deposits, milestone billing, faster collections, or a reserve policy.

## Limitations

Cash flow analysis depends on accurate and current financial data. KORA should avoid giving formal financial advice and should recommend professional support when stakes are high.
