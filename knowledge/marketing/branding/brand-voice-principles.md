# Brand Voice Principles

## Summary

Brand voice is the consistent way a business sounds across messages, content, sales conversations, documents, and public presence.

It matters because consistency builds recognition and trust. A clear voice helps different outputs feel like they came from the same source.

## Core Idea

Brand voice should define:

- personality;
- level of formality;
- vocabulary;
- rhythm;
- emotional tone;
- what the brand says;
- what the brand avoids saying;
- how the brand adapts by channel.

Voice is not decoration. It is a trust system.

## When To Use

- Creating content, landing pages, proposals, PDFs, emails, scripts, and support messages.
- Reviewing whether communication feels consistent.
- Turning brand strategy into practical writing rules.
- Training agents or humans to write in a recognizable style.

## When Not To Use

- When factual accuracy, legal language, or compliance is more important than expression.
- When there is no defined audience or positioning.
- When voice is being used to hide an unclear offer.

## How To Apply

Use a voice profile:

```text
We sound: [3 to 5 traits]
We avoid sounding: [3 to 5 traits]
We use words like: [vocabulary]
We avoid words like: [forbidden or off-tone words]
In short content, we are: [channel adaptation]
In long documents, we are: [channel adaptation]
In sales messages, we are: [channel adaptation]
```

Review any output by asking:

1. Does this sound like the brand?
2. Is it clear to the audience?
3. Does the tone match the stakes of the moment?
4. Is the voice helping trust or distracting from meaning?

## Examples

Voice profile example:

```text
We sound: precise, helpful, direct, calm.
We avoid sounding: inflated, vague, pushy, overly casual.
```

## Limitations

Voice must adapt to context. A sales page, legal notice, onboarding email, and social caption should not sound identical, but they should feel related.
