# Funnel Stages Model

## Summary

A funnel describes how a person moves from first attention to trust, decision, action, and follow-up.

It matters because marketing problems are often stage problems. A business may not need more traffic if the real issue is unclear offer, weak trust, poor follow-up, or low lead quality.

## Core Idea

Useful funnel thinking separates the journey into stages:

1. Awareness: the person notices the business, idea, problem, or offer.
2. Relevance: the person recognizes that it may apply to them.
3. Trust: the person sees proof, consistency, authority, or familiarity.
4. Consideration: the person compares options, cost, risk, and fit.
5. Conversion: the person takes the next step.
6. Follow-up: the business responds, qualifies, nurtures, sells, or retains.

Each stage needs a different message and metric.

## When To Use

- Diagnosing why a campaign, landing page, profile, ad, or content system is not converting.
- Planning a lead generation path.
- Mapping content to business outcomes.
- Deciding whether to improve traffic, offer, proof, CTA, or follow-up.

## When Not To Use

- When the buyer journey is too simple to require funnel mapping.
- When no real offer or audience has been defined yet.
- When the problem is operational delivery rather than marketing flow.

## How To Apply

Map the current path:

```text
Where do people first see us?
What tells them this is relevant?
What makes them trust us?
What question or objection appears before action?
What action do we ask for?
What happens after they act?
```

Then identify the weakest stage and improve one stage at a time.

## Examples

High profile views with low contacts may point to weak relevance, unclear offer, weak CTA, or insufficient proof.

Many contacts with low sales may point to poor lead quality, unclear qualification, slow response, price mismatch, or weak follow-up.

## Limitations

Funnels are simplifications. People may loop, skip stages, return later, or convert through offline trust.
