# Marketing Metrics Reading

## Summary

Marketing metrics should be interpreted as signals about a system, not as isolated scores.

A single number rarely explains performance. Useful interpretation connects attention, fit, trust, conversion, and follow-up.

## Core Idea

Read metrics by stage:

1. Attention: are enough relevant people seeing the message?
2. Engagement: are the right people showing interest?
3. Clarity: do they understand the offer or idea?
4. Conversion: are they taking the next step?
5. Quality: are leads or customers a good fit?
6. Retention or follow-up: does value continue after the first action?

Do not optimize a late-stage metric before checking earlier-stage constraints.

## When To Use

- Reviewing campaigns, social content, landing pages, ads, funnels, or lead generation systems.
- Diagnosing why a marketing channel is underperforming.
- Turning raw numbers into decisions.

## When Not To Use

- When there is not enough data to infer a pattern.
- When tracking is unreliable or definitions are inconsistent.
- When qualitative research is needed before metric analysis.

## How To Apply

Use this diagnostic sequence:

1. Define the goal.
2. Map the funnel or user path.
3. Identify the metric for each stage.
4. Look for the first major drop-off.
5. Compare quantity and quality.
6. Form one hypothesis.
7. Change one meaningful variable.
8. Measure again.

## Examples

High reach but low clicks may indicate weak relevance, unclear promise, poor CTA, or wrong audience.

High clicks but low leads may indicate landing page mismatch, weak offer clarity, lack of trust, slow page, or too much friction.

Many leads but few sales may indicate poor lead quality, weak qualification, pricing mismatch, or sales follow-up problems.

## Limitations

Metrics can hide context. Platform changes, seasonality, sample size, tracking errors, and audience quality can distort interpretation.
