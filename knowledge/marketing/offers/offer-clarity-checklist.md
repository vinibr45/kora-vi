# Offer Clarity Checklist

## Summary

An offer is clear when the buyer can quickly understand what is being sold, who it is for, what outcome it promises, what is included, what is not included, and what action to take next.

## Core Idea

People hesitate when an offer leaves too many unanswered questions. Clarity reduces cognitive load, perceived risk, and unnecessary back-and-forth.

An offer should make the buying decision easier by clarifying:

- audience;
- problem;
- outcome;
- deliverables;
- process;
- proof;
- price or pricing logic;
- timeline;
- guarantees or risk reversal;
- next step.

## When To Use

- Reviewing a service page, proposal, landing page, product description, or sales message.
- Turning vague services into packaged offers.
- Diagnosing why leads ask basic questions before buying.
- Preparing content that explains an offer.

## When Not To Use

- When the offer itself is still being discovered through research.
- When the problem is traffic quality rather than offer clarity.
- When the buying decision requires a custom consultation by design.

## How To Apply

Review the offer with these questions:

1. Is the target buyer explicit?
2. Is the main problem or desire named in buyer language?
3. Is the promised outcome concrete?
4. Are deliverables listed plainly?
5. Is the process easy to visualize?
6. Are common objections answered?
7. Is there proof or credibility?
8. Is the price, range, or pricing logic clear enough?
9. Is the timeline or effort expectation clear?
10. Is the next step obvious?

If three or more answers are unclear, revise the offer before increasing traffic.

## Examples

Unclear:

```text
Marketing consulting for your business.
```

Clearer:

```text
A 2-week offer audit for local service businesses that need to turn website visitors into qualified WhatsApp leads. Includes positioning review, landing page diagnosis, message hierarchy, and a prioritized action plan.
```

## Limitations

Some premium or complex services require qualification before details are finalized. In those cases, clarity should focus on fit, process, proof, and next step rather than a rigid package.
