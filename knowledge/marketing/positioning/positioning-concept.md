# Positioning Concept

## Summary

Positioning defines how a business, product, service, or person should be understood by a specific audience in relation to alternatives.

It matters because people do not evaluate an offer in isolation. They compare it against existing categories, familiar options, perceived risks, urgency, trust, and expected outcomes.

## Core Idea

Good positioning answers five questions:

1. Who is this for?
2. What problem or desire does it address?
3. What category should the audience place it in?
4. Why is this different or preferable?
5. What outcome should the audience expect?

Positioning is not a slogan. It is the strategic frame that makes messaging, offer design, content, and sales conversations easier to understand.

## When To Use

- Before creating a landing page, campaign, offer, or content strategy.
- When an audience does not immediately understand what something is.
- When a product or service is being compared to the wrong alternatives.
- When messaging feels generic, vague, or interchangeable.

## When Not To Use

- When the task only needs execution against an already clear positioning.
- When the audience, offer, and category are unknown and still need research.
- When the issue is delivery quality rather than market understanding.

## How To Apply

Use this sequence:

1. Identify the target audience.
2. Name the current alternative they use or consider.
3. Define the category frame.
4. State the core problem or desired progress.
5. Identify one or two meaningful differentiators.
6. Translate that into messaging hierarchy.

Useful output format:

```text
For [audience],
who struggle with [problem/desire],
[offer] is a [category]
that helps them [outcome],
unlike [alternative],
because [differentiator].
```

## Examples

Weak positioning:

```text
We create beautiful websites.
```

Clearer positioning:

```text
We build conversion-focused websites for local service businesses that need more qualified WhatsApp leads without managing complex marketing tools.
```

## Limitations

Positioning should be tested against real audience language, competitor alternatives, and sales objections. Internal clarity is useful, but market response matters more.
